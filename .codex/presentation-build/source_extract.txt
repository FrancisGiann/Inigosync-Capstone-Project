P99	Body of Research	This study developed IñigoSync, a web-based booking management system for Iñigos Sports Center in Lucena City. It addressed problems caused by managing court reservations through calls, social media messages, walk-in inquiries, and manual records, which resulted in overlapping schedules and difficulties in tracking bookings. IñigoSync enabled customers to view court information and rates, check available schedules, reserve courts, and monitor booking statuses. Staff users managed customer records, online and walk-in bookings, payment records, and receipts. The administrator monitored business performance and staff activities and configured the customer arrival grace period. The system automatically cancelled unattended bookings after the configured grace period. The developers followed the Agile methodology and developed the system using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with Supabase and PostgreSQL for backend services and database management. It was deployed through Hostinger, while PayMongo payment processing was tested using simulated transactions in a sandbox environment. Developer testing, adviser evaluation, and user testing identified navigation difficulties, database connection errors, and responsiveness concerns that guided system improvements. The study concluded that IñigoSync provided a more organized and accessible platform for managing reservations, schedules, payments, and customer records while supporting business and staff monitoring.

P125	Heading 2	Project Context
P126	Normal	The use of web-based booking systems had become important in service-based industries, particularly in sports and recreational facilities, because these systems supported scheduling, real-time monitoring of facility availability, and customer access. Through online platforms, users could reserve services at preferred times, reducing the need for in-person inquiries. Booking systems also helped organize operations and records and reduce manual errors (Febriansyah & Waluyo, 2023; Daza et al., 2026). Digital transformation had been recognized as a strategy for improving operational processes and service delivery. The Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 emphasized the adoption of digital systems to streamline processes and improve access to services (National Economic and Development Authority, 2023). In addition, the Department of Trade and Industry (2023) highlighted the importance of digital technologies for small and medium enterprises, including online platforms for scheduling, reservations, and service management.
P127	Normal	Sports centers and recreational facilities faced challenges in managing reservations because of their reliance on traditional booking methods, such as walk-in reservations, phone inquiries, and manual logbooks. These practices could lead to double bookings, fake bookings, overlapping reservations, and inefficient record-keeping. For Iñigos Sports Center, manual coordination created difficulties in monitoring available courts and maintaining organized reservation records. These issues made booking management more difficult for staff and customers.
P128	Normal	The developers addressed these concerns by developing a web-based booking management system intended to improve accessibility, administrative control, and the organization of daily operations.
P129	Normal	Ms. Rosalinda Driz, the secretary of Iñigos Sports Center in Brgy. Bocohan, Diversion Road, Lucena City, had assisted with the business since 2011. Since that year, the sports center had accommodated walk-in customers who used the facilities without prior appointments. At the time of the interview, Ms. Driz managed reservations and inquiries primarily through the official social media account of Iñigos Sports Center, where customers sent messages to check available slots and request bookings. Despite this approach, booking coordination remained largely manual and became more difficult as the number of customers increased.
P130	Normal	As a solution, the developers developed IñigoSync to provide a centralized platform for managing court reservations and improving access to booking information. The name combined the sports center’s identity with the concept of synchronization, reflecting the system’s purpose of coordinating schedules.

P303	Normal	According to Asana (2025), Agile methodology is a project management approach that breaks down software development into smaller, iterative phases known as sprints. In Scrum, daily stand-up meetings, also known as Daily Scrum, are conducted to monitor progress, discuss ongoing tasks, and identify obstacles that may affect sprint completion. These sprints allow teams to perform key processes such as requirement gathering, design, development, testing, and evaluation within a short period. The developers will begin by identifying and prioritizing specific tasks or features from the project backlog, followed by planning how these tasks will be completed within the given timeframe. The development phase will involve coding and building functional components, while continuous testing will be conducted to immediately detect and fix errors. Upon completion of each sprint, the developers will conduct a review and evaluation phase, where the output will be presented to end-users for feedback. This feedback will then be used as a basis for improvements and adjustments in the next sprint cycle. This iterative approach enables continuous improvement, flexibility, and active collaboration with end-users, making Agile methodology highly suitable for projects that require adaptability and frequent feedback.
P304	Normal	The developers will adopt the Agile methodology in developing IñigoSync: A Web-Based Booking Management System for Iñigos Sports Center due to its iterative and flexible approach, which aligns with the project’s objectives and the operational requirements of the facility. Currently, Iñigos Sports Center encounters recurring issues such as overlapping reservations, difficulty in monitoring schedules, and the absence of a centralized booking system, resulting in operational inefficiencies and possible inconsistencies in managing reservations. Through Agile, specifically the Scrum framework, these challenges will be addressed progressively through incremental development. Each sprint will have a duration of approximately 10 to 15 days, enabling the proponents to concentrate on specific system features, gather user feedback, and incorporate necessary refinements in subsequent iterations. Key functionalities—including user registration, facility reservation, schedule management, and booking confirmation—will be developed in separate sprints and evaluated using a local server to ensure accuracy, reliability, and overall system performance.
P305	Normal	Compared to other software development methodologies, Agile is the most appropriate approach for this project. Unlike the Waterfall methodology, which follows a rigid and sequential process, it is not suitable since the system requirements may evolve during development and will require frequent client feedback. Implementing changes in Waterfall after the development phase begins will be difficult and costly. On the other hand, the Spiral model is more appropriate for large-scale and high-risk systems that require intensive risk analysis, which is unnecessary for a localized booking management system like IñigoSync.

P611	Body of Research	IñigoSync was deployed through Hostinger and connected to Supabase for database management. The administrator and staff received training and a user guide to support the use of the system. During the pilot operation, the system operated alongside the existing manual process. The implementation results indicated that the staff could perform the required tasks and that the client confirmed the system’s readiness after final evaluation.
P616	Body of Research	Conclusion
P617	Body of Research	The development of IñigoSync accomplished the study’s goal of providing a web-based platform for managing court reservations at Iñigos Sports Center. Customers could view court information, select available schedules, and complete the booking process, while the administrator and staff could manage reservations, walk-in transactions, payment records, and reports. By bringing these functions into one system, IñigoSync provided a more organized way to monitor court availability and maintain booking information. Its scheduling features supported the prevention of overlapping reservations and the management of cancelled time slots.
P618	Body of Research	The testing and revision process contributed to the improvement of the system’s functionality and usability. Feedback from the adviser, client, and student testers helped the developers identify problems that affected navigation, schedules, records, and interface responsiveness. Addressing these findings produced an improved version in which the main features operated as intended during testing. The successful PayMongo test transactions also demonstrated that the system could update booking statuses and generate receipts after simulated payments. These payment findings were limited to the sandbox environment used during the study.
P619	Body of Research	The online deployment, staff training, and pilot operation supported the sports center’s preparation to adopt IñigoSync. Training and the user guide helped the administrator and staff understand the new booking workflow and complete the required tasks. Together with the client’s reported confirmation of readiness, these findings supported the system’s suitability for initial operational use.
